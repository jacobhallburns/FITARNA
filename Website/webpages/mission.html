Our Mission

Bringing augmented reality navigation indoors — making every building as easy to navigate as an open map.

"To develop an accessible, real-time augmented reality indoor navigation system that empowers students, faculty, and visitors at Florida Tech to find their way through complex indoor environments with confidence and ease."
— FITARNA Senior Design Team, Florida Institute of Technology

The Problem

GPS technology has transformed outdoor navigation, but it fails entirely the moment you step inside a building. Multi-story academic buildings, labs, and event centers can be genuinely disorienting — especially for new students, guests, or anyone with accessibility needs.

Traditional solutions like printed maps and static directory boards are easy to miss, hard to update, and provide no turn-by-turn guidance. FITARNA addresses this gap by using the camera already in every smartphone to overlay live navigation cues directly onto the real world.

Project Goals

📍

Real-Time Positioning

Use AR Foundation and on-device sensors to accurately determine a user's location and orientation within a mapped building.

🗺️

Turn-by-Turn Guidance

Overlay directional waypoints and route highlights onto the camera feed, guiding users to any room or point of interest.

📱

Cross-Platform Support

Deliver a seamless experience on both iOS (ARKit) and Android (ARCore) devices through a shared Unity codebase.

♿

Accessibility First

Design navigation paths and UI elements with accessibility in mind, supporting users of all abilities.

⚡

Low-Latency Experience

Ensure AR overlays remain stable and responsive even in challenging lighting conditions typical of indoor spaces.

🏫

Campus Integration

Map Florida Tech buildings with accurate floor plans so the system can be deployed and extended to new buildings over time.

How We Get There

FITARNA is built on Unity's AR Foundation framework, which provides a unified abstraction layer over ARCore (Android) and ARKit (iOS). This lets the team write shared C# logic while still leveraging each platform's native AR capabilities.

Floor-plan data is served via a TypeScript backend, which the app queries at runtime to load the correct map for the building being navigated. A pathfinding layer computes the optimal route and converts it into a series of 3D waypoints that are anchored to real-world coordinates and rendered as AR overlays.
